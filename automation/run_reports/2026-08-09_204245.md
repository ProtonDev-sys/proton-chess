# Pawn-safe minor mobility

## Problem

The evaluator counted every pseudo-legal knight and bishop destination as mobility, including squares controlled by enemy pawns. This rewarded pieces for nominal activity that would usually lose material immediately and made the mobility term less like the way a human assesses usable squares.

## Change

- Build the ordinary pseudo-attack map exactly as before.
- Use the complete map for attack aggregation and king-safety terms.
- For knight and bishop mobility only, remove destinations in the opposing pawn attack map before applying the existing mobility weights.
- Leave rook, queen and king mobility unchanged.

This is pawn-safe pseudo-mobility rather than legal mobility. A pinned pawn still suppresses a destination, which is a deliberate inexpensive approximation rather than a move-generation rule.

## Focused regressions

- One enemy-pawn-controlled knight destination reduces the tapered evaluation by exactly 4 cp.
- One enemy-pawn-controlled bishop destination reduces it by 5 cp after tapering.
- Rank/color mirrors produce identical scores for both pieces.
- A rook destination controlled by the same pawn geometry remains counted, guarding the intended minor-only scope.
- The full attack map remains untouched by construction; the change cannot alter legal moves or attacks used outside the mobility count.

The evaluation shift changed several seeded limiter fixtures. They were retuned while preserving their original assertions. The tight loss-band test was strengthened:

- The observed full depth-5 search chooses `Qb6` at `+46`.
- The observed fresh `b6`-only search scores `+10`, 36 cp worse and outside the 22 cp allowance.
- With skill 19, seed 27 and `HumanMaxLossCp=40`, the skill adjustment produces a 50 cp effective allowance; two consecutive restricted selections are `[b6, Qb6]`.
- With `HumanMaxLossCp=12`, the same adjustment produces a 22 cp effective allowance. That search followed by the wide allowance on the same RNG stream returns `[Qb6, Qb6]`.
- The second sequence proves the first draw sampled `b6`, confirmation rejected it, and the draw was consumed; simple prefiltering would have left the first wide result at `b6`.

An independent audit repeated the control and rejection sequence in 12 fresh processes with one unique result.
